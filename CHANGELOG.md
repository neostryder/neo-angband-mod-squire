# Changelog

All notable changes to this mod are recorded here. Versions follow the mod's own
`manifest.json`, which is what the game reads, and each released version has a
matching git tag that an install pins itself to.

An entry has to matter to somebody running the mod. Documentation wording,
internal refactoring and test-only additions are not recorded here. Bug fixes
are, however small.

An entry opens with one or more bracketed tags. `[Visible]` marks a change a
player would notice in the game or mod itself; `[Internal]` marks one that
touches only code, tooling, or a maintainer's own workflow, with nothing for a
player to see. A further tag (`[Security]`, `[Balance]`, `[UI]`, `[Modding-API]`,
`[Localization]`, `[Save-Compat]`, `[Docs]`, `[Content]` or `[Compatibility]`)
names what kind of change it is. Lists appear in this
order and each is omitted when empty for a release: Added, Changed, Removed,
Fixed.

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- [Visible] **Squire's model is told how its character works.** A Mage hears that it casts with Intelligence, a Necromancer that lit ground spoils its spells, a Blackguard that its mana comes from hitting things. Birth options that change the game, like no up staircases or no selling, go in too. Reading them takes one new permission: the game's options.
- [Visible] **Squire works toward goals at three ranges.** It picks a long-term goal from its persona's pursuits, such as winning or going deeper than the family ever has, and that goal pulls harder on its choices. It works on one mid-term aim at a time, like a spellbook or a lantern, and picks a step for the level it's on, like exploring, resting or taking the stairs. The panel shows the step and the aim, and the new Show the long-term goal setting adds the long one. Each goal that ends goes into the run report as met, failed or dropped.
- [Visible] **Squire rethinks its mid-term aim when something big happens.** A new artifact or ego item, a new rune or spell, drinking half its healing potions, using its last escape scroll or teleport staff charge with no reliable escape spell to fall back on, nearly dying, a trip into or out of town, each new five levels of depth, a dangerous unique in view, or the player letting Squire play again each make it ask its model whether to drop the aim and pick another, and it checks every 150 actions too. Dropping or selling potions and scrolls doesn't count as using them. A dropped aim can't be picked again for 2,000 game turns, and a stubborn persona needs a firmer yes. Squire drops at most two aims on each dungeon level over a whole run, so leaving a level and coming back doesn't reset the count. The panel's aims section is now an Intent card: the current step, the aim it serves, the first thing the next level down asks for, and when the next check comes.
- [Visible] **Poison with no potions left is no longer a death sentence.** Squire can now gamble on a potion or scroll it hasn't identified, or head for the stairs and the shops that sell Cure Light Wounds. Before, a poisoned Ranger with nothing to drink couldn't plan a walk anywhere, and swung at thin air until it died.
- [Visible] **Squire walks away from a creature that never moves.** Once the monster memory shows that a creature does not chase intruders, the creatures line says it never moves and a step out of its reach counts as a safe retreat.
- [Visible] **Fight decisions now ask whether to escape.** Whenever an escape, a heal or an untried potion or scroll is on offer, the escape question goes out with the move choice. Above about 0.78, Squire only considers its escapes and heals; between about 0.55 and 0.78 it also allows healing, an untried potion or scroll, or a better square to fight from. Bold characters need a higher answer before they break off. When the model is unsure of its move, Squire runs if the escape answer was 0.5 or more and otherwise makes its safest attack or waits.
- [Visible] **Squire notices when it goes in circles.** Making the same choice three times with nothing changing, pacing over a few squares, going up and down between two levels, putting an item back on, buying back what it just sold, coming back to the same part of a level with nothing new to show for it, a command sequence that keeps failing, and stepping around without getting closer to the goal all count. Squire names the failed choice and why it failed to the model and asks once more, and if the answer is the same it picks something else itself.
- [Visible] **Fight options say what they cost and what they're for.** Next to each option the model reads a note such as "serves survival; progress: most of the way; costs 1 of 3 carried; some risk", cut down to whatever differs between the options. A step toward the current near-term goal is marked as one.
- [Visible] **Two new persona sliders: Law to Chaos and Good to Evil.** Law to Chaos sets how often the character strays from its best-rated option and how readily it changes goals. Good to Evil sets what it's willing to do, from sparing the harmless to stealing and harming townspeople, and the voice of its chronicle. Both start at 50, and a persona saved before they existed loads at 50. Like the other traits, a strong setting goes to Squire's model with the persona.
- [Visible] **Style presets carry a play rule.** The diver, the completionist and the paranoid join the six presets already there. Each preset now gives the model one short rule: the coward retreats at the first real loss of hit points, the diver ignores minor loot and heads down, the completionist maps the whole level and keeps identify scrolls, and the paranoid won't fight a unique without an escape path. The persona sheet's new Play style picker sets or clears the rule without touching the sliders, and changing a slider by hand keeps it.
- [Visible] [UI] **A character you've just made opens Squire's panel on the Persona tab.** The presets are offered there before Squire first plays it, and a new install's persona starts neutral. The Default choice for a new persona is now called Neutral.
- [Visible] [Balance] **Law to Chaos now changes what Squire picks.** Once the safety limit and the escape question have had their say, Squire draws its move from the options left instead of always taking the one rated highest. A lawful character always takes the top option, a neutral one now and then takes the next best, and a chaotic one strays often. Long-term, mid-term and near-term goals are drawn the same way, and the log names each pick that left the top option.
- [Internal] **The decision log names the goal Squire took on its own.** When the model picks none of the offers in danger, the record now says which survival goal Squire chose instead. A plan's ending also counts its refused commands by kind, such as walk or cast.
- [Internal] **The decision log keeps the escape answer and its band, the pruned options, the unsure-answer fallback and any loop found.** Laya gets the escape question as its own training row beside the goal row, over the same state.
- [Internal] **The decision log keeps each Law to Chaos draw.** It records the method, the sampling temperature or the dice rolled, the top option and the pick. A stored setting with no panel control, chaosMethod, can swap the draw for a d20 that keeps the top option on a high enough roll and otherwise lets a d4 choose among the top four options.
- [Visible] **Every way out of a fight is its own option.** Phase Door no longer stands in for an escape spell, and Teleport Level, stairs right next to the character and healing sit beside the other escapes instead of behind them. The model sees the cost and risk of each. Spells, wands, rods and staffs give their chance to fail.
- [Visible] **Squire can send a creature away.** With a scroll, wand or rod of Teleport Other it can aim at an awake creature it has a clear shot at. That counts for most when the creature is the only one awake.
- [Visible] **Squire can duck out of sight.** It finds squares a step or two away that no awake creature in view can see and walks there. Facing a unique, it waits a turn on that square and hits the unique when it comes round the corner.
- [Visible] **In a fight the model gets the ground up close.** Instead of the whole level it hears what is on each square next to the character, where it can get out of sight, how many turns away the stairs are, where the nearby choke points are, and any burning ground or traps within five steps.
- [Visible] **Squire ends a plan the game won't carry out.** When the game refuses a command, a step leaves the character where it stood, or an object is still on the floor after Squire picks it up, the plan stops there and the next decision hears what went wrong.
- [Visible] [Balance] **Squire looks again after drinking, reading, zapping, throwing, firing, casting or taking stairs.** If that left its hit points in a lower band, gave it poison, confusion or another status, or woke a creature it can see, it drops the rest of the plan and asks the model again.
- [Visible] **Rests, runs, walks and exploring stop for a fixed list of events, and never stop to ask the model.** Each one stops for a new hostile creature, a change in hit points or status, a new message, an object coming into view, a staircase, a failed command, a side passage on a run or an exploring walk, and a target that moves on a walk toward it.
- [Visible] **A command checks its target just before it goes.** If the scroll it was meant to read is gone, the spell can't be cast anymore or the creature it was aimed at is out of sight, Squire cancels the command instead of letting it land on something else.
- [Visible] **Options that run into a birth option say so.** With connected stairs off or forced descent on, the option to go down says what that means for the character, Word of Recall options mention no-recall, and a shopping trip mentions no-selling.

### Changed
- [Visible] [Balance] **Backing away or moving to a better square stops after three steps.** Squire looks at the fight again from there. A walk to the stairs still goes all the way.
- [Visible] **Options that another option beats outright are dropped.** Before asking, Squire removes an attack, escape or heal when another of the same kind is at least as good on risk, cost and progress, such as throwing oil at an orc that one dagger blow would kill, or aiming a wand when Magic Missile does more for less. When one option is left, Squire takes it without asking. The exception is a descent the character isn't ready for: Squire then goes back to its own rules for the next move.
- [Visible] [Balance] **An heir inherits sixteen family traits and rolls the rest.** Mercy, Renown, Good to Evil, Law to Chaos, Greed, Ambition, Honour, Devotion, Gratitude, Resentment, Pack weight and Home use, plus the hated, feared and distrusted lists and the superstitions, move toward the parent's values by as much as the Family resemblance setting allows. Boldness and the other temperament sliders, the tactics and money sliders, favoured spells and weapons, and quirks are rolled fresh. Lineage settings and Squire's own dials, such as Persona strength and Trait drift, come from the persona marked for new characters.
- [Visible] **A blood grudge can now call for caution as well as vengeance.** With the killer of a family member in view, the model is told how many of the family it killed, whether the family hates or fears it, and that either response fits. The persona decides.
- [Visible] [Balance] **Going deeper with something missing is a judgment call now.** Squire used to refuse the stairs down until the character passed every check for the next depth. Light, food and at least half the levels and hit points that depth asks for are still required. Past those, Squire asks its model whether the character is ready anyway, and a cautious persona needs a firmer yes than a bold one. When no answer comes back, the character stays on its level.
- [Visible] [Balance] **Each creature's danger rating counts that creature alone.** A crowd of townspeople used to make every one of them deadly to a level 1 character, and a floating eye looked as dangerous as the wild dogs beside it. A creature whose blows Squire hasn't seen is rated on an average round instead of its worst one. The damage estimate for the next action still counts everything.
- [Visible] **Attacks go at the creature that can come for the character.** With a floating eye next to it and wild dogs closing in, Squire used to offer to fight the eye. It now picks an awake creature that moves first, and a sleeping or stationary one only when nothing else is around.
- [Visible] **What Squire sends with each decision starts with the character's health, the creatures in view and the damage coming in, and ends with the game's rules.** Laya's copy is held to 1,024 tokens by dropping the lessons first and the rules last, and so is the copy Laya gets for training while Jev plays. Mana and damage numbers come with a word for their size, like low.
- [Visible] [Balance] **Breeders cost fewer interruptions and Squire leaves them sooner.** One more worm of a kind already counted no longer stops a plan when its bite would take less than a tenth of the character's hit points. Leaving the level is offered once four breeders of one kind are in sight, down from six.
- [Visible] **Thrown oil and attack spells list their damage.** A flask of oil reads as about 7 damage when it hits, and Magic Missile shows its damage next to its chance to fail, so the model can weigh them against a sword.
- [Visible] **Numbers sent to the model come with a word for their size.** A creature reads as 2 steps away (close), a depth as dungeon level 4 (shallow) and the character as Level 5 (novice), so the model doesn't have to guess what a bare number means.
- [Visible] **Questions to the model spell out what they ask.** The escape question gives the character's hit points and the damage it could take, the readiness question names the level and what the character lacks, an option's purpose names the near-term goal it serves, and the in-character question points at the persona entry by name.

### Fixed
- [Visible] **A retreat never steps onto a trap the character can see.** Squire used to back away onto a known trap when that square was the furthest from the creature.
- [Visible] **An order never to teleport covers every teleport.** "Never teleport" or "don't use teleportation" now rules out Teleportation, Teleport Level and escape spells, where it used to rule out only Phase Door. "Never phase" rules out Phase Door, whether read or cast.
- [Visible] [UI] **The Retreat threshold and Ambition sliders label their ends correctly.** Retreat threshold now runs from "holds out" to "leaves early", and Ambition from "content" to "set on winning". The old scales had two ends joined by a second "to", so the persona sheet showed the wrong labels and a cowardly character was described to the model as "very the end".
- [Visible] **A creature that stops an errand starts its sentence with a capital letter.** The status line used to read "cave spider came into view."
- [Visible] **Squire only fears the spells it has seen.** It used to count every spell a kind of creature can cast, so a creature the character had never met was already feared for magic nobody had watched it use. Its spells now count once the monster memory shows it casting or breathing.
- [Visible] **Race and class protection counts toward the deep levels.** A Dwarf cannot be blinded, yet Squire still sent it looking for blindness protection before dungeon level 56. Protection from blindness and confusion now counts whether it comes from race, class or a worn item.
- [Visible] **No more trips down on a dying torch.** Whenever its other choices had run out, Squire could leave town, or Recall back down, with a single torch and nothing to replace it. Going down now takes a light that never runs out, or a lit one plus a spare torch or flask of oil. Stairs taken to escape are exempt.
- [Visible] **Laya plays with Squire's trained model.** With the Model name setting empty, Squire asked Laya's base model, so a trained Squire model on the server was never used. It now asks for `laya:squire_goal`, and a server without that model answers with its base model as before.
- [Visible] **Squire sells at every shop that buys.** The Bookseller, Magic Shop and Black Market were taken for the home, so a spare wand, ring or book never made it to the store that buys it. Each shop now goes by its own name, and the Black Market is a stop only for goods no other store buys.
- [Visible] **Squire leaves floating eyes alone.** It never offers to fight one in melee and never walks into one on an errand, so oil, arrows, spells or walking away are what's left. A creature whose memory says it can paralyse gets the same treatment, unless the character wears Free Action. If the character loses several turns in a row, Squire stops the errand, marks whatever stood next to it as a creature not to strike, and decides again.
- [Visible] **Long walks stay away from creatures and burning ground.** The game's own travel command walks a whole route with no check along the way. Squire now uses it only when the route keeps clear of every creature in view, asleep or awake, and off ground known to burn. On the way to the stairs the game picks which staircase to walk to, so every known staircase needs a clear route. Otherwise it walks one step at a time and checks each one.
- [Visible] **An errand stops once a creature in view can kill the character before its next action.** Exploring, shopping, fetching loot and taking the stairs are no longer offered at all while that's true, unless the stairs are underfoot. Poison and bleeding don't count here, so a poisoned character low on hit points can still walk to the stairs. A level 1 Mage no longer walks past a mean-looking mercenary that can deal 10 damage in one round.
- [Visible] **Squire stops exploring a town it has already walked.** It remembers each square of town it has stood on during a visit, so a town with no new ground left and no known stairs no longer sends it round the same streets again.
- [Visible] **Squire lights a torch before it tries to study.** Studying needs light, so a Mage with an unlit torch and a new spell to learn used to try the spell first, get refused, and only then light the torch.
- [Visible] **Squire sees the game's messages in the middle of a level again.** The first part of Squire to read each batch of messages used it up, so level feelings, newly learned runes and the arrival notes for a new level never reached the rest.

## 0.2.0 - 2026-10-01

### Added
- [Visible] **Squire fights with what works and keeps its way out.** When one blow can kill, it takes the finishing attack; otherwise it compares melee, arrows, oil, wands and spells by damage per action. It won't spend the mana, oil or wand charge its next escape depends on, and a young character leaves the level once three breeders are awake around it. Swapping gear never costs the Free Action, See Invisible or resistance the next depth requires.
- [Visible] **Six more family touches.** A family motto passes down and an heir repeats it at a level-up or a narrow escape, or drops it for its own when its persona differs a lot. An heir favours the weapon kind its most successful ancestor used. The depth where an ancestor died unsettles a cautious heir and draws a proud one. Level-ups and first unique kills get a line in the persona's voice, a proud character sometimes boasts of a first kill, and an ancestor's artifact is recognised when it turns up again in a shop or on the floor.
- [Visible] [Balance] **Each Squire character chases the goals its persona cares about.** Winning the game (Sauron on dungeon level 99, then Morgoth on 100) is a goal for every character, weighted mostly by Ambition, beside goals the persona picks: riches, fine gear, seeing the dungeon, uniques, a family grudge, an ancestor's deepest level, depth for its own sake, and keeping the family line alive. A miser can care more about gold than about winning, and a tourist more about seeing each level.
- [Visible] [Balance] **Town trips and shopping follow the persona.** While supplies are still above the level that forces a trip, a character with a high Town trips slider turns back sooner, and a driven one carries fewer spare potions and stays down. In town, once it holds two healing potions and two Phase Door scrolls, a character with high Savings keeps the rest of its gold and a treasure hunter spends it on gear. Patience sets how often it waits for Word of Recall instead of exploring. Each character's goal weights also drift a little from its persona's, so siblings with the same persona still play differently.
- [Visible] [Balance] **Squire buys gear upgrades and sells what it no longer needs.** In town it buys a weapon or armour piece it can afford when it is clearly better than what the character wears, unless the persona chose to save its gold this visit. It sells spare wands, rods, rings, amulets and supplies beyond what it carries, each at a shop that buys that kind of item, and keeps one of each kind of device, three rods of Curing or Healing, and its best attack wand. The Selling and Pack weight sliders decide how much goes. It never sells or stores what the character is wearing, its last light, its escapes and Word of Recall, or the two healing potions and two Phase Door scrolls it always carries.
- [Visible] [Balance] [Save-Compat] **Squire keeps a stockpile in the home.** It leaves spare potions, scrolls, flasks, torches and food there, and takes from the home before buying in a shop. It learns what the home holds by going in, and remembers it with the character, so the stockpile is still known after a reload.
- [Visible] [Balance] **Squire steps to a defensible square before a hard fight.** When a summoner, a breather or two or more awake creatures are in sight, it can walk up to four steps to a corridor, doorway or dead end where fewer of them can reach it at once, along a route that costs no more than standing still, and wait there one turn for them to come. Cautious, patient and self-preserving personas take the spot more readily than bold or impulsive ones, and it never walks past a dangerous creature to get there.
- [Visible] [UI] **Squire shows why a fixed errand ended.** The reason appears in the autoplayer status, such as "Stopped: cave spider came into view."
- [Visible] [UI] **The retreat line and the length of a short errand are settings.** The retreat line runs from 10% to 90% of maximum hit points and a short errand from 50 to 500 decisions; the defaults stay at 50% and 200.
- [Visible] **Heirs remember who killed the family.** An heir hates or fears the creature that killed its ancestors, depending on its persona, and more strongly with each death. Hatred pulls it toward the fight and can make a hated unique an aim; fear makes the creature look more dangerous and offers a way off the level, and neither overrides the safety floor. Killing a hated unique settles the grudge, and feelings about an ordinary monster fade over the generations unless it has killed three of the family.
- [Visible] **Squire checks the danger before it moves.** It estimates the damage coming over its next action or two, only counts an escape that leaves it safer, checks every step of a walk to the stairs, and heals with the smallest cure that keeps ahead of the damage, or a weaker one when that is all it has near death. When a single cure or escape is all that keeps it alive, it takes that without waiting on the model.
- [Visible] **Squire gets ready before it goes deeper.** It leaves town with food, light and a healing and Phase Door reserve, checks its level, hit points and supplies against each new depth first, heads home while it still has what it needs to get there, and stays near the stairs while it is young. Once it is ready for the next depth, it searches the whole level for the way down.

- [Visible] **Squire plays the whole game when a model is set up.** Press Ctrl-Z and Squire asks Jev, Laya or another System One server what to do each time something changes, and plays on until you take the keyboard back. It fights, shoots, throws oil, aims wands, casts attack and healing spells, drinks healing potions, reads Phase Door and Teleportation, backs away, rests, eats, picks things up, learns new spells, wears better gear, maps a new level with a scroll, rod or spell, explores, takes the stairs, and leaves a level once worm masses or other breeders fill it. In town it buys healing, escapes, food, light and oil, and it reads Word of Recall to restock and go back down. Squire uses items only by the names you can see and reads a shop's stock only while standing in it.
- [Visible] [UI] **The Squire panel.** A dockable panel with six tabs. Setup picks the model and any backup Laya servers to try when the first is busy, stores its key, tests the connection and sets spend limits. The others are Persona, Lessons, Dashboard (a depth chart and the latest decisions), Orders and Report. Its text grows with the window, and the A- and A+ buttons change its size.
- [Visible] **Personas.** Sliders, favourite and hated lists, quirks and a backstory shape how Squire plays, starting from Default, a random persona or an archetype such as coward, berserker or miser. Squire asks for the best move and the in-character move and blends them, and a safety floor keeps it out of fights it would certainly lose unless the persona has a death wish or an order it follows closely says otherwise. Personas can be exported and imported.
- [Visible] **Knight's Lessons.** While you play, Squire forms its own choice at each moment that matters, notes where yours differed and learns your style. It issues no commands. It offers "Why, sir?" reasons and "Watch this" demonstrations, rises through three ranks, infers a persona from your play that you can save, gives exams, and can show a hint when it disagrees.
- [Visible] **Run reports.** When a character Squire played dies, wins or retires, the Report tab shows the outcome, depth, top kills, uniques, lessons, the apprenticeship and tokens used, and saves the report as Markdown or JSON, a share card image, or the decision log, which keeps the latest 20,000 decisions.
- [Visible] **Each decision in the log shows what came of it.** When a plan stops, its entry notes whether it ran to the end, got interrupted or gave you back the keyboard. It also counts the commands sent and any the game ignored, and gives the hit points gained or lost. You'll find this in the saved log, and it goes out with telemetry if you send every decision.
- [Visible] **Fewer model calls.** Squire no longer asks the model about moves with only one sensible answer. With no awake creature in sight it lights a torch, wears a known upgrade, learns a spell, reads a detection scroll on a new level, or waits for a recall to fire. If only one safe move is left, it takes it, and while nothing changes it repeats the model's last choice for a few turns. The decision log lists these as reflexes, and they use no tokens.
- [Visible] **Lessons now say what went wrong.** When a battle-scarred veteran hits a level 1 mage for 8 as it walks up, the lesson reads "prefer range or avoid it below 16 HP" instead of "Nearly died after choosing to fight". Squire learns this way from heavy blows, paralysis and other disabling attacks, breath and spells it sees, attacks a creature shrugs off, breeders and escapes that fail. It never blames resting, and killing a unique no longer adds a lesson.
- [Visible] **Another look at the exits.** If the model turns down every option while the character is in trouble, Squire adds the escapes it had held back, such as the nearest stairs, and asks once more.
- [Visible] **Squire sizes up a creature by what it knows of it.** Once the character has seen a creature's blows, spells or breeding, those set its rating, so a level 1 mage treats a battle-scarred veteran that can hit for 12 a round as dangerous before the first blow lands. Low hit points, a crowd close by and a faster foe raise the rating further. Squire also casts the attack spell that does the most damage for its mana, holds its fire when a wall or another creature is in the way, and aims ball spells where they catch the most creatures.
- [Visible] **Refused moves aren't offered again right away.** If the game won't carry out a move, Squire leaves it out until the character steps elsewhere, its health or status changes, or a creature moves. When it can tell why, such as confusion, it says so to the model.
- [Visible] **Roll-on.** After a death, Setup can have Squire start the next character, like the last one or with a random race and class, and keep playing. The new character inherits some of what its ancestors learned.
- [Visible] [Security] **Telemetry, off by default.** Setup lists what each level sends to squire.rpgm.tools, from a run summary up to every decision, and a backstory is sent only with its own consent. "Delete what I have sent" asks the service to delete this install's records.
- [Visible] **Train Laya while Jev plays.** When switched on in Setup, Squire also sends each of Jev's questions to Laya as its own request, skips one while Laya is still answering the last, and never acts on Laya's answer. Every Jev decision is saved as a training row, and a Knight's Lessons row carries your own choice as its label. "Save Laya training rows" downloads them.
- [Visible] **Squire keeps shooting at the creature it picked.** Arrows, flasks of oil, wands and bolt spells go on at one target until it dies, leaves sight, the line of fire closes or the ammunition or mana runs out, with no new model call per shot. A ball spell is still aimed afresh each cast, so the blast stays off the character. With nothing in view, Squire now walks to stairs and unexplored ground with the game's own travel and run commands, and when it flees it heads for a known staircase before it just backs away.
- [Visible] [UI] **Squire works toward aims.** On each new level, after a town trip, on gaining a level and every 2,000 game turns, Squire lists what it wants next (its next spellbook, a lantern, armour for empty slots, a magic weapon, free action, see invisible and a target depth) and has the model rank them. Moves that serve an aim say so and count for a little more with an ambitious persona, and a recall to town comes up once an aim is affordable. The list shows under Aims in the Squire panel.
- [Visible] [UI] **Give Squire orders.** Write an order ("reach 500 ft before level 15") or a standing instruction ("always run from uniques") in the new Orders tab, or press Ctrl+Shift+O while the panel is open. Squire weighs each one in every decision, and how readily it obeys depends on its Devotion, Resentment and persona: a devoted squire may follow an order past its usual risk limit, and a proud one follows grudgingly. Instructions fade over time, faster for a Forgetful squire. Creed files save and load a set of standing instructions, a family creed passes to heirs, and "Instructions kept" in Setup caps how many it holds.
- [Visible] **Squire uses more of what it finds.** It judges floor items before walking to them, drops junk when the pack is full, and on a town trip buys the item of an aim it can now afford. Before a hard fight it drinks Heroism, Berserk or Speed or reads Blessing, it drinks a resist potion before a known breather, and it uses curing devices and activations. It disarms traps, tunnels through rubble that blocks its way, and leaves a level whose feeling is bad.
- [Visible] **Heirs carry on the family's aims, and orders have their own key.** An heir inherits its predecessor's depth target and a weapon the line was hunting, as strongly as the Inheritance slider allows, and an unambitious heir can drop a deep depth target; aims tied to the old character's kit start fresh. Squire's order key (O, or N when O is taken; none if both are) opens the order prompt during play whether or not the panel is open. An order Squire liked, or one it followed and was thanked for, now builds its Gratitude.
- [Visible] **Viewers can give Squire orders.** With a "Viewer orders address" from Squire Link in Setup, Squire collects orders from Twitch or Discord chat every few seconds while it plays and weighs each as a viewer's request, which counts for less than your own orders unless the squire is very devoted. The Orders tab and journal name the viewer who gave each one.
- [Visible] **Epitaphs.** The log names the killer, depth and last choice when a character dies. Family memory keeps recent epitaphs for heirs.
- [Visible] **Family milestones.** The family records depth records, first unique kills and its first artifact; heirs recall shared depths and uniques in the log.
- [Visible] **Namesakes.** Heirs can take numbered ancestor names, favouring ancestors who reached deeper or lived longer.
- [Visible] **Inherited superstitions.** Heirs avoid the scroll, potion or wand used just before an ancestor died; identifying its kind ends the fear.
- [Visible] **Lessons of the dark.** A squire whose ancestor died without light can buy an extra Wooden Torch or Flask of Oil before leaving town.
- [Visible] **Trophies.** Proud characters keep one item from each slain unique while the pack has room.
- [Visible] **Favoured grounds.** Heirs favour exploring at the depth of an ancestor's most valuable find once their gear and supplies let them reach it.

## 0.1.0 - 2026-09-11

First release.

### Added

- [Visible] [UI] **Squire runs one bounded errand and then hands the keyboard back.** Three errands ship: engage one creature and stop, walk out the unexplored part of a floor and stop, and a longer errand that carries the character until there is nothing left to do on the floor. Which short errand runs is read from the world at handover, so handing over with something in sight fights it and handing over in an empty corridor explores. An errand that ends stops issuing commands, and the game's own hatch gives the keyboard back on the next key pressed.

- [Visible] [UI] **Every short errand ends on a disturbance rather than playing through one.** A creature that was not in sight arriving, a loss of hit points, a status effect landing, the floor changing underfoot, and death all end an errand on the decision they happen. The exploring errand ends on the first point of damage; the fighting errand keeps going and ends at half hit points instead, since damage during a fight is the fight. None of it is read from message text, so the conditions hold in every language.

- [Visible] [UI] **Eight settings, each a labelled toggle.** Three choose which errands are offered, two choose which disturbances end one, and three cover attacking sleeping creatures, picking things up, and taking the stairs down during the long errand.

- [Visible] [Balance] **A sleeping creature is never chosen as a target on its own.** Waking something the character had walked quietly past is a decision a player makes; a target the player set with the game's own targeting command is honoured whether it is asleep or not. The behaviour can be switched on.

- [Internal] [Modding-API] **Routing plans only over ground the character remembers.** Paths are poured as a breadth-first flow field over remembered, walkable, non-burning ground, and the only move that is not planned that way is a step into an unexplored grid next to the character. The map view's floor-object count reports the live floor rather than the player's knowledge, so nothing routes toward it and collecting is limited to what is underfoot.

- [Internal] [Modding-API] **Terrain is classified from the bound feature registry by flag rather than by code.** A mod's own staircase, door or burning ground is recognised on exactly the same terms as the base game's. A missing registry degrades to an errand that walks the floor without taking stairs or opening doors, and says so in the log.
